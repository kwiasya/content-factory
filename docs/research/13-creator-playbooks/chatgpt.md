# C: Cross-Creator Commonalities & Contrasts

| **Agreed (common)**                             | **Contradictions (creative differences)**                              |
|-------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Strong Hook:** Nearly all creators start with an immediate hook (question, demo or high stakes) to earn attention early.  | **Pacing:** Some (MrBeast, Fireship) use hyper-fast cuts and energetic SFX, while others (Johnny, Kurzgesagt, MKBHD) use slower, more cinematic pacing. |
| **Narrative Structure:** Most build a clear narrative or game structure (challenge outline, question-answer, tutorial steps) to guide the viewer.  | **Face vs. Faceless:** Johnny Harris and Casey are on-camera hosts, whereas Kurzgesagt and Fireship use animation/graphics (no host face). Veritasium mixes both. |
| **Value & Quality:** All emphasize delivering promised value and high production values (good audio-visual quality). They target engagement (not mere clickbait). | **Length and Style:** For example, MrBeast experiments with very long formats (50+ min live streams), whereas Jenny Hoyos focuses on <1 min Shorts. This partly reflects platform norms (Long-Form vs. Short-Form). |
| **Use of Emotion/Stakes:** Many incorporate suspense, surprise or charity (MrBeast’s giveaways, Yes Theory’s fear/challenge) to elicit emotional investment. | **Scripted vs. Spontaneous:** Creators differ: Casey’s vlogs feel ad-libbed, vs. Kurzgesagt’s meticulously scripted animation. Some report these differences anecdotally (PRACTITIONER) rather than tested. |

**Evidence:** Synthesis from the MrBeast guide, creator interviews/analysis and platform trends. Commonalities are well-documented (e.g. the value of hooks and storytelling), while contradictions (pace, presentation style, length) are noted by creators themselves and commentators.

# E: Deprecated Tactics & Flops

- **Abandoned tactics:** MrBeast himself rejected some early tactics.  By 2024 he admitted the era of “ultra-fast paced/overstim” editing was over. Similarly, Casey Neistat largely abandoned daily vlogging after 2018, focusing on larger projects (he noted burnout in interviews).  Many creators once thought serial challenge formats guaranteed success, but even MrBeast warned against endless copycats.

- **Flopped imitations:** Numerous clones of MrBeast’s giveaway/challenge style failed to go viral because they lacked scale or novelty. (For example, many small channels tried “last to leave” contests without the huge budgets; none matched MrBeast’s millions of views.)  Analyst Anthony Padilla observed that “MrBeastification” led to copycats obsessed with metrics, often with poor results.

- **Retired formats:** Kurzgesagt originally covered a broader range of topics (some early videos were more casual) but refined their focus to “big science/philosophy questions.”  FIREship’s founder (Jeff Delaney) pivoted from fast tech news to tutorial shorts as the market changed.  In practice, many large creators evolve: what worked in 2020 may be shelved if data shows dropping retention (as seen when Harris slowed editing pace in 2026). Negative findings highlight that slavishly following old formulas leads to diminishing returns.
