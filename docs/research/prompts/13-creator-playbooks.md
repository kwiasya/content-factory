CONTEXT (same for every research task in this series, 07–14)

I am a solo creator making videos for YouTube (long-form and Shorts), TikTok and
Instagram Reels, in whatever format fits the idea: talking head, voice-over on footage,
screencast, POV walks with music only, vlog, challenge. I am building an AI agent
(Claude Code: markdown "skills" plus Python) that writes video scripts, proposes hooks,
titles and structure, and critiques a draft script before I shoot or edit. This research
series collects the evidence-based rules that agent will follow. Platform priority:
YouTube first, TikTok second, Instagram third.

Research rules:
- Source hierarchy, best to worst: (1) statements, docs and published research from
  YouTube / Google, TikTok and Meta; (2) creators showing their own data (retention
  graphs, A/B thumbnail tests, analytics screenshots, internal documents);
  (3) analysts working from samples (Paddy Galloway, Colin and Samir, VidIQ, 1of10,
  Film Booth, Jon Youshaei and similar); (4) peer-reviewed research on attention,
  curiosity, suspense, emotion and narrative. Exclude marketing blogs, SEO listicles
  and paid-course content unless they quote a tier 1–4 source; then cite that source.
- Tag every rule or claim with its evidence level: DATA (a measurement over a sample,
  an A/B test or a retention graph is shown; one video's view count is not DATA),
  PRACTITIONER (a creator's or analyst's stated practice, no data shown) or THEORY
  (research or a model, not tested on video). Keep the three apart.
- Time horizon: for platforms, algorithms and creator case studies prefer 2024–2026
  and flag anything older as possibly outdated. For psychology, emotion and
  storytelling theory there is no date limit; classic sources are welcome.
- Examples must be concrete: a named video with a link and a timestamp (or a named
  creator and a named video), never "many creators do X". A timestamp taken from a
  transcript, a chapter list or a published breakdown is fine; say where it came from.
- Be explicit about what does NOT work, what is contested, and what is a myth that
  the sources themselves have retracted. Negative findings are valuable.
- Do not pad. Tables over prose. Cite a link for every claim.

RESEARCH TASK 13: what top creators say they do, checked against what their videos show

Goal: the stated playbooks of the creators who define the craft in 2024–2026, verified
against their own videos, compared with each other, and filtered for what a single
person with no team and no budget can actually do.

Required sources to work through:
- MrBeast: the leaked internal onboarding document for his team (2024; state its
  authenticity status and who confirmed it; paraphrase, quote only short phrases), his
  interviews (Lex Fridman, Colin and Samir, Joe Rogan, Jay Shetty, The Diary of a CEO
  and any 2025–2026 ones), Paddy Galloway's analyses of his videos, and his own public
  statements about changing his style in 2024–2026 (moving away from hyper-fast
  editing, longer and calmer videos).
- Comparison schools, one block each: Johnny Harris (narrative journalism), Veritasium
  (misconception-first explainers), Kurzgesagt (animated explainers), Fireship
  (fast tech explainers), MKBHD (tech review), Jenny Hoyos (Shorts), Casey Neistat
  (vlog), Drew Binsky and Yes Theory (travel), Ryan Trahan (challenges).
- Add up to 5 creators of your own choice if they represent a school not covered
  above; justify each addition in one line.

Find and document:

1. MrBeast: the actual rules in the internal document (the first minute, the retention
   checkpoints, "wow factor", critical components, what he calls boring), each with
   the quote or paraphrase and the page; his interview statements; then take 3 of his
   videos from different years and show each rule in action with timestamps. Then what
   he changed in 2024–2026 and why, in his words.
2. Each comparison creator: stated principles (from interviews and their own "how I
   make videos" material), their typical structure, hook habits, retention devices,
   packaging approach, what is unique to them; one video with timestamps that shows it.
3. Cross-creator: what every school agrees on; where they contradict each other (fast
   cuts versus slow pacing, face versus faceless, scripted versus improvised, length);
   where the only evidence is self-report.
4. Solo transfer: for each principle, can one person with no team and no budget do it
   (yes / partly / no), and what the substitute is when the answer is partly or no.
5. Negative findings: cargo-cult copies of these playbooks that failed (with examples),
   and principles the creators themselves abandoned or contradicted later.

Output format:
- Table A: creator × principles (hook, structure, retention, packaging, emotion) ×
  source link.
- Section B: the MrBeast document distilled: rule, source (page or timestamp),
  example timestamp in a video, still valid in 2026 (yes / changed / dropped, with his
  own statement).
- Table C: what is common across schools versus what contradicts, with who says what.
- Table D: principle × transferable to a solo creator (yes / partly / no) × substitute.
- Section E: what they stopped doing and what copies failed.
