CONTEXT (same for every research task in this series, 07–14)

I am a solo creator making videos for YouTube (long-form and Shorts), TikTok and
Instagram Reels, in whatever format fits the idea: talking head, voice-over on footage,
screencast, POV walks with music only, vlog, challenge. I am building an AI agent
(Claude Code: markdown "skills" plus Python) that writes video scripts, proposes hooks,
titles and structure, and critiques a draft script before I shoot or edit. This research
series collects the evidence-based rules that agent will follow. Platform priority:
YouTube first, TikTok second, Instagram third.

Research rules:
- Source hierarchy, best to worst: (1) statements, docs and published research from
  YouTube / Google, TikTok and Meta; (2) creators showing their own data (retention
  graphs, A/B thumbnail tests, analytics screenshots, internal documents);
  (3) analysts working from samples (Paddy Galloway, Colin and Samir, VidIQ, 1of10,
  Film Booth, Jon Youshaei and similar); (4) peer-reviewed research on attention,
  curiosity, suspense, emotion and narrative. Exclude marketing blogs, SEO listicles
  and paid-course content unless they quote a tier 1–4 source; then cite that source.
- Tag every rule or claim with its evidence level: DATA (a measurement over a sample,
  an A/B test or a retention graph is shown; one video's view count is not DATA),
  PRACTITIONER (a creator's or analyst's stated practice, no data shown) or THEORY
  (research or a model, not tested on video). Keep the three apart.
- Time horizon: for platforms, algorithms and creator case studies prefer 2024–2026
  and flag anything older as possibly outdated. For psychology, emotion and
  storytelling theory there is no date limit; classic sources are welcome.
- Examples must be concrete: a named video with a link and a timestamp (or a named
  creator and a named video), never "many creators do X". A timestamp taken from a
  transcript, a chapter list or a published breakdown is fine; say where it came from.
- Be explicit about what does NOT work, what is contested, and what is a myth that
  the sources themselves have retracted. Negative findings are valuable.
- Do not pad. Tables over prose. Cite a link for every claim.

RESEARCH TASK 09: storytelling structures for long-form and short-form video

Goal: the structures that keep people watching, as beat templates with time budgets, so
the agent can outline a video and check a draft against a structure. I already have a
taxonomy of storytelling formats and editing styles (below); validate and extend it.

My current storytelling formats (keep, rename, merge, split or add; justify each):
a_vs_b_comparison, about_me_origin_story, breakdown_explainer, case_study, challenge,
common_trap_mistake, contrast, day_in_the_life, episodic_series_social_show,
heros_journey, interview_clips, levels (level 1 to level N, each better than the last),
listicle, personal_learning_epiphany, personal_update, pov, problem_solution, q_and_a,
ranking_rating_tier_list, reaction, reveal (builds toward one thing shown last),
scenario_hypothetical, skit_humor, tutorial, yap (one take, talking it out, no script).

My current editing styles (same request), grouped: Studio/Set: car_yap, office_room_yap,
whiteboard, split_screen, full_screen_hybrid, comparison_clone, podcast_clips.
Greenscreen: visual_greenscreen, notes_article_greenscreen, reaction. In-World vlog:
vlog_hybrid, vlog_interactive, vlog_pov, vlog_music (cut to a track, no voice),
vlog_timelapse, vlog_reflective, man_on_street_single, man_on_street_multiple.
In-World skit: skit_solo, skit_group, skit_lip_sync, skit_transformation. Faceless:
faceless_visual_explainer, faceless_animation, faceless_clipping,
faceless_gameplay_narration, faceless_physical_explainer, faceless_reaction_explainer,
faceless_text_conversation. Other: stop_motion.

Find and document:

1. Narrative frameworks top YouTubers actually use, with where and how: hero's journey
   compressed, the "but / therefore" rule (Trey Parker and Matt Stone), Dan Harmon's
   story circle, three acts, stakes → escalation → payoff, MrBeast's "every minute
   better than the last", Johnny Harris's question → journey → answer, Veritasium's
   misconception-first model, Kurzgesagt's explainer structure, tutorial structures.
   For each: who uses it, evidence beyond self-report, an example with timestamps.
2. Stakes and tension in non-fiction: kinds of stakes (money, time, risk, reputation,
   curiosity, personal), how they are introduced and escalated, how often; evidence.
3. Payoff design: delivering on the promise; periodic payoffs versus one final payoff;
   "reward schedules"; where the biggest payoff should sit; what happens to retention
   after a payoff and how creators handle it.
4. Structure by length: 60 s, 3 min, 10 min, 20+ min. What changes in beats, time
   budget per beat, and number of open loops. Give templates with timings.
5. Structure without narration and without a face: music-only vlogs, text-on-footage,
   screencasts, animation. How progression, stakes and payoff are carried visually and
   by on-screen text; examples with timestamps.
6. Taxonomy validation: for my storytelling formats and editing styles, which are real
   recurring structures with evidence, which overlap, which are missing; what the
   analysts' own taxonomies look like (Sandcastles, Hook Genius, 1of10, others) and how
   they differ from mine.
7. The writing craft: outlining practices, scripted versus semi-scripted, how creators
   test a structure before shooting (table reads, predicted retention, beat sheets),
   tools and templates they use.
8. Negative findings: structures that read well on paper and fail on video (context
   dump first, backstory first, "in this video I will"), and contested advice.

Output format:
- Table A: frameworks (name, who uses it, beats, evidence level, example + timestamps,
  best for which length).
- Section B: beat templates per length (60 s / 3 min / 10 min / 20+ min) with a time
  budget per beat and the open loops in play at each point.
- Table C: taxonomy verdicts for every one of my formats and editing styles (keep /
  rename / merge / add, reason, evidence).
- Section D: stakes and payoff rules, each tagged DATA / PRACTITIONER / THEORY.
- Section E: myths and structures that fail.
