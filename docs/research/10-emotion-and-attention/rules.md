## Emotion budget

Long-form:
- Open: curiosity plus relevance; A adds a brief surprise.
- Setup (C): trust, rules, first micro-payoff.
- Middle: tension, evidence, partial relief, renewed question. A keeps arousal up; C warns against constant stimulation.
- Reversal (C): surprise that changes the causal model.
- Pre-payoff (C): focused suspense, side loops closed.
- Payoff: relief, surprise beyond the promise.
- End: A stays on the peak; C adds a calm meaning beat first.

Short-form:
- Open: surprise or a legible gap.
- Middle: one causal line, small joy rises.
- Payoff: clear answer, relief, amusement or awe.
- End: A high-arousal positive; C aftertaste. Both: stop after delivery.

## Refuted and myths

- Three open loops, gap closes in X seconds: no source (A, C).
- Pattern interrupt every X seconds: unsourced (A, C).
- Faster edit always better: Lang 1999; Tam via C.
- Zeigarnik memory advantage: Ghibellini & Meier 2025.
- Mozart effect: Thompson 2001.
- Cliffhanger raises intent to continue: Wirz 2022.
- Mehrabian 7/38/55 for voice-overs: Mehrabian's own caveat.
- Face improves understanding: Kizilcec 2014.
- Fans are lonely: Tukachinsky 2020.
- Working memory 7 ± 2: Cowan 2001.
- Arousal causes sharing: Prowten 2024 replications.
- Pause before punchline: Attardo & Pickering 2011.
- Six arcs as templates: Reagan's caveats (A, C).
- Demotion cost watch time: Goodrow 2021 reports +0.5%.
- Named rage-bait penalty, TikTok weights: unverified (C).

## Disagreements

- Gerrig: A THEORY, C DATA; A better, a measured text study.
- Arousal and sharing: A cites a failed replication, C cites support; A better.
- Cuts: A Lang 2000 supports jump cuts, C a 2025 VR study against; both lab, no platform data.
- Authenticity: A phone-native look, C no evidence; C better.
- Speech rate: A about 170–190 wpm from radio, C no number; C safer.
