### 4.D Where sharing and watch time conflict

| Lever | Helps sharing | Helps continued watching or reading | Conflict and rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sadness (low arousal) | Hurts: −16% per SD (4.12) | Hurts: 52% vs 80% continue (4.16) | Raises **clicks** (+0.7% per SD, 4.15). Use it in the packaging or the final beat, never as the mood of the middle. |
| Certainty and closure | More likes and shares (4.16) | Less sustained attention (4.16) | Close loops only at the end. Mid-video certainty ends the session. |
| Anxiety, fear | Anxiety +21% (4.12) | Anxious wording 80% continue (4.16) | Fear words **lower** headline CTR (−0.7%, 4.15). Keep worry inside the video (stakes) rather than in the title. |
| Anger | +34% (4.12); out-group animosity strongest (4.17) | 71% continue (4.16) | No effect on clicks (4.15). It carries satisfaction and regret costs, covered in item 9. |
| Information density | Hurts sharing of ads (4.14) | Easy-to-process language helps reading (4.16) | Practical value helped NYT sharing (+30%, 4.12). The conflict is about form: useful works, dense works against you. |
| Amusement, awe, excitement | Raise sharing (4.12, 4.14) | Joy velocity retains (4.8); excitement and hope keep reading (4.16) | No conflict found. These are the safest emotions for both goals. |
| Ending | The end effect on sharing is the strongest (4.7) | MrBeast: payoff, then abrupt end (4.26) | Compatible: end on the peak and cut. Do not add a low-arousal outro after the payoff. |
| Platform | IG names reshare as a top prediction (4.22) | YouTube: valued watch time (4.19); Shorts: shares are sparse (4.20, 4.21) | For Reels, include one share trigger at the payoff. For YouTube, optimise continuation first. |

### 4.E Negative and contested

- **The arousal → sharing mechanism did not replicate.** Two direct replications of Berger (2011) (N = 111 and N = 160) found that incidental physiological arousal did **not** raise willingness to share news on social media. The field correlations (4.12) stand, but "arousal itself causes sharing" is now doubtful. DATA (lab). Prowten et al., *Psychological Science* 35(9):1025–1034 — https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/09567976241257255/ (2024-09). [verified]
- **The arc studies never measured video retention.** Reagan uses book downloads, which the authors call a "rough proxy". Del Vecchio uses box office, and its winning arc rates *lower* with audiences. The Berger papers use text. The only moment-to-moment video evidence is from ads (4.7, 4.8), and it is 2012–2016.
- **Contagion through text is negligible** (d ≤ 0.02, 4.11), so "the viewer feels what the caption says" is unsupported. Contagion through face and voice is plausible (4.10, 6.8) but untested on platform video.
- **Practitioner claim "any emotion works" (4.25)** contradicts three datasets on sadness and certainty (4.12, 4.15, 4.16).
- **"Shares are the main Shorts signal"** is contradicted by a YouTube product lead and by Hoyos's own correlation analysis (4.20, 4.21). It may still be true on Instagram (4.22, 4.23), and only secondhand evidence exists for the 2025 weighting.
- **The negativity bias in clicks applies to discrete emotions in a specific way:** sadness helps clicks, while fear and joy hurt them (4.15). "Negative = clicks" is too coarse a rule.

### 5.A What can stand in for a face (ranked by the strength of the evidence)

1. **A natural human voice.** Voice alone carries perceived competence (5.9, the face added nothing), humanness (5.10) and PSR (5.8). DATA (lab or survey; not tested on short video).
2. **Verbal direct address** ("you", second person, questions to the viewer). In 5.3 it contributes to PSI on its own, though the effect is strongest combined with bodily address. DATA (lab).
3. **Self-disclosure** in the voice-over or on a text card (5.7). DATA (survey).
4. **Similarity and homophily signals:** the author's own life, ordinary places, everyday people (5.4, 5.6, 5.19). DATA (meta and survey) plus PRACTITIONER.
5. **A brief face at turning points** where it is acceptable (the hook, the reveal, the end). The talking-head interleave beat slides-only (5.11), and the face raises affect without improving recall (5.12). DATA (education video).
6. **Consistency across videos** (series, recurring phrases, the same voice), which builds PSR by repetition. THEORY (5.2) plus PRACTITIONER (5.16). No video data was found.

### 5.B Negative and contested

- **The Mehrabian "7/38/55" rule is misapplied.** Film Booth says voice-overs work because "38% of communication … is vocal" (*The Silent Hack YouTubers Use To Hook You* — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IaWRqyOtUUQ, 3:57–4:07, auto-caption track, 2022-03-23, possibly outdated). Mehrabian himself says the equations come from experiments on communicating feelings and attitudes (like–dislike) and "unless a communicator is talking about their feelings or attitudes, these equations are not applicable" (http://www.kaaj.com/psych/smorder.html). The voice-matters conclusion survives on other evidence (5.9); the percentage does not. [verified: Film Booth quote at 3:57–4:07 and Mehrabian's note both checked]
- **"Open palms … proven easier on the eye"** (same Film Booth video, 2:11–2:38) is stated without a source. PRACTITIONER, unsupported. [verified: the quote is at 2:19–2:23]
- **Comment activity does not build the bond** in uploaded video (5.5). Replying to comments may still help through fairness (5.6), which is a different mechanism.
- **The lonely-fan myth** is not supported (5.4).
- **A face does not raise comprehension** (5.12). The case for a face is affect and retention (5.11), not learning.
- **The education-video evidence (5.11, 5.12) is long-form instruction from 2014.** No platform-level 2024–2026 data with numbers on face versus faceless retention was found (see Gaps).
- **Authenticity versus drama (5.21)** is unresolved: the micro-drama and reality formats (4.27) live on manufactured conflict, while relationship-led creators avoid it.

### 6.A Negative and contested

- **"Pause before the punchline"** is not supported by acoustic measurement (6.5). The supported timing rule is to leave silence *after* it (6.4).
- **Humor costs credibility** (6.10). [unconfirmed: rests on 6.10, which is now under Refuted or unconfirmed] Do not put jokes on the claims the author needs believed, such as safety tips or prices.
- **Canned and fake laughter:** spontaneous laughter beats posed laughter (6.7), and laughter from an out-group can backfire (6.8).
- **Agreement-laughter (clapter)** is the cheapest kind and builds a caricature (6.15). PRACTITIONER only.
- **The memory advantage is relative** (6.12). Wall-to-wall jokes lose it.
- **Callbacks:** no study testing callbacks on audience response was found. The rule rests on practice and on the setup–payoff memory logic only.
- **Humor for watch time** (as opposed to sharing) has no direct video evidence. The sharing evidence (6.9) is solid, and the retention case rests on joy velocity (4.8) and on practitioners (6.14).

## Gaps

1. **No platform data from 2024–2026 links an emotional arc to retention.** Every arc result is from books, film scripts, text or 2012–2016 ad studies. No YouTube, TikTok or Meta source publishes emotion-by-retention curves.
2. **No 2024–2026 source with numbers was found on face versus faceless retention, or on faces in Shorts and Reels.** The face evidence (5.11, 5.12) is MOOC video from 2014. TikTok's and Meta's own creative guidance on "talk to camera" could not be located after the search budget ran out.
3. **The Mosseri 2025 "sends per reach" statement** was found only in secondhand summaries (4.23).
4. **Conflicts between sources:** sadness helps clicks (4.15) but hurts sharing (4.12) and continued reading (4.16). Fear hurts clicks (4.15) while anxiety helps sharing and reading (4.12, 4.16). Anger has no effect on clicks but helps sharing. The arousal-sharing mechanism failed replication (Prowten 2024). Practitioners (4.25) say the type of emotion does not matter, which the data contradict.
5. **Shares:** a YouTube product lead and Hoyos say shares are a sparse or weak signal on Shorts (4.20, 4.21), while Instagram names reshare as a top prediction (4.22). The emotion budget has to be set per platform.
6. **Not re-verified this session** (fact-check: Kizilcec, Platow, Schmidt and Dibble are now verified; Kowert is a conceptual paper; Eisend and Zillmann remain unconfirmed and were moved to the end) (paywalled or abstract elided; DOIs given): Kizilcec 2014 (5.12), Kowert & Daniel 2021 (5.13; title only), Platow 2005 (6.8), Eisend 2009 (6.10), Schmidt 1994 (6.12), Zillmann 1971 (6.2), Dibble 2016 (5.2), and Provine 1992 (6.6; title only).
7. **Callbacks and comedic timing in edited video:** no empirical study was found. A theory of callbacks as in-group signals (Flamson & Barrett's "encryption theory of humor", 2008) exists, but its DOI could not be confirmed, so it is left out.
8. **Upload dates not retrieved** (fact-check: now retrieved. The Creator Insider video is 2024-12-09 and *Trapped On An Island* 2026-03-21): the Creator Insider Hoyos/Sherman conversation (dated to about 2023 from the features it discusses) and MrBeast *Trapped On An Island* (171M views, late in the channel list). YouTube's bot wall stopped further metadata fetches.
9. **Emotional contagion through on-screen faces and voices on short-form platforms** is untested. The only field test of contagion (Kramer 2014) is text-only and found negligible effects.
