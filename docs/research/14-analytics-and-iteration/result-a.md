# 14 · Analytics and iteration: reading retention, programmatic access

Source: Claude Opus research agents (3 researchers, 3 fact-checkers, 1 writer), 2026-10-12. Notes: notes/.

## How to read this file

- **Tags.** **DATA**: a measurement over a sample, an A/B result or a shown graph. **PRACTITIONER**: a stated practice with no data shown. YouTube, TikTok and Meta Help text and staff statements show no data, so they are PRACTITIONER and marked **[T1]**. **THEORY**: research, statistics or a model not tested on video. **DOC**: a tier 1 platform *specification* (what an endpoint returns, a limit, an access rule), checked against the page text. In CONTEXT terms DOC is a platform statement with no sample, so it sits with PRACTITIONER [T1]. The notes kept it as its own tag for API facts, and this file does the same. **Inference** marks a rule the researchers derived that no source states.
- **[T1]–[T4]** give the tier in the CONTEXT source hierarchy. **Old** means before 2024 and possibly outdated.
- **Timestamps.** `[tx]` means read from the video's YouTube auto-captions during research. Caption-block timestamps in these channels run 10–20 s late (fact-check in note 07), so start about 15 s earlier. Blackman "Retention Review" (RR) timestamps are his own, taken from his written breakdown. His posts link to channels, not videos, so those examples are a named creator, a named video and his timestamp.
- **No web search ran** for this prompt: the shared search budget was used up before the researchers started. Sources were reached by fetching known first-party URLs and transcripts already cached in the session. See Coverage.
- **This prompt asks for no taxonomy verdicts** (hook categories, formats, editing styles, layouts), so none appear here.

### B3. Contested and negative (diagnosis)

- **Early-drop reading after the 2026 view change.** vidIQ reads its 54% engaged-view ratio as "the hook failed almost half of the time" ([We8TVsjs2B8 11:17–11:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=We8TVsjs2B8&t=677) [tx]). The notes' counter-reading (inference): a hover-play that never commits is closer to an impression than to a rejected hook. YouTube has not documented whether the retention curve, Intro % and APV use all views or engaged views, so the first ~10 s of a post-2026-08-24 curve should not be compared with older curves.
- **First-hour CTR as a predictor.** Galloway: CTR "as a whole is not very useful", but first-hour or first-24 h CTR "can be a good predictor" on established channels (claimed, not shown; [Creator Science #209, 53:07](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/paddy-galloway-2/), PRACTITIONER [T3]). YouTube: early data is not decisive (B1.6). Both can hold; no public dataset tests it.
- **Turning off notifications or embeds "to protect metrics".** Beaupré "wouldn't advise" turning notifications off and says non-predictive sources carry little weight (B1.8). Some creators claim it helps; no data either way.
- **"A dud tanks the channel."** Denied at the algorithm level ([wrIlifWnKDs 06:06–06:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wrIlifWnKDs&t=366) [tx]); true only through audience memory (A5.5).
- **Deleting viral off-niche videos** to fix the audience: Beaupré "wouldn't advise deleting videos" ([wrIlifWnKDs 01:36–02:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wrIlifWnKDs&t=96) [tx]).
- **Suggested-traffic lists as proof that "the algorithm misunderstands my audience"**: wrong; watch-page suggestions reflect each viewer's interests, not topical relatedness ([rHLjxrbXmmY 28:58–30:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY&t=1738) [tx]).
- **Curve shape → cause pairings D6–D12** rest on n=1 practitioner readings, mostly Blackman 2022–23 (**Old**, small and mid channels). Treat them as hypotheses to test, not established rules.

### D4. Realistic confidence for a channel with a few thousand views per video

| # | Claim | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| D4.1 | Impressions **per variant** to detect a CTR difference (80% power, α = 0.05, n ≈ 16·p(1−p)/δ², baseline 4%): 4.0 → 5.0% (+25% relative): **~6,100**; 4.0 → 4.4% (+10%): **~38,400**; 4.0 → 4.2% (+5%): **~154,000**. | THEORY (arithmetic on Miller's rule) | Miller |
| D4.2 | **Worked case**: ~3,000 views per video at ~4.5% CTR with ~70% of views from impressions ≈ 45–50k impressions. Three arms of ~15k detect only ≥ ~16% relative CTR differences; two arms of ~23k detect ≥ ~13%. Test & Compare decides on watch time per impression, which has higher variance (many zero-watch impressions), so the real limit is coarser. **Only large, deliberately different variants can produce a Winner; expect Inconclusive for subtle ones.** Consistent with YouTube's own finding that subtle thumbnail differences showed no difference ([j7GtybYc6gk 5:24–5:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7GtybYc6gk&t=324) [tx]). | THEORY (arithmetic; 70% and 4.5% are assumptions) | Miller; [Help 16391400](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en) |
| D4.3 | **Inconclusive ≠ no difference.** An underpowered metric hides real effects (Microsoft example: detectable only ≥7.8% at 80% power). A significant result on an underpowered metric **exaggerates** the effect (winner's curse). | THEORY [T4] | Dirty Dozen §5.4 |
| D4.4 | Treat a borderline p-value as a possible false positive and re-run. | THEORY [T4] | Dirty Dozen §5.5 |

### D7. Contested and negative (experiments and loop)

- **Swap-and-watch-realtime is confounded.** Veritasium watched for "a noticeable bump in views" after a change; his asteroid video went from 9th of his last 10 on day 1 to his best (14M) after a day-3 title change ([S2xHZPH5Sng 9:01–9:52, 10:28–10:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2xHZPH5Sng&t=541) [tx], **Old**; one case, not DATA). YouTube says sequential comparison is unreliable (D2.1). Beaupré: a title or thumbnail change triggers no re-index or boost ([HP-rqigfw2s 32:04–33:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HP-rqigfw2s&t=1924), via note 12).
- **CTR as the test metric.** YouTube's tools reject it (D1.2); Hanif: in "a sea of metrics", CTR distracts ([j7GtybYc6gk 12:04–12:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7GtybYc6gk&t=724) [tx]). Veritasium (2021, **Old**): title and thumbnail are "at least half the job" and you must optimize CTR and watch time ([S2xHZPH5Sng 7:18–7:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2xHZPH5Sng&t=438) [tx]).
- **Winners decay.** Thumbnail fashions flip: an orange win gets copied, then blue wins ([HP-rqigfw2s 31:08–31:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HP-rqigfw2s&t=1868) [tx]); re-runs flip (D1.6). Novelty effects inflate short tests (Dirty Dozen §5.10).
- **How much to test pre-publish.** Galloway values full-video A/B because once uploaded "there's very little we can actually do to test" ([j7GtybYc6gk 7:17–7:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7GtybYc6gk&t=437) [tx]). MrBeast (2025-01-29) has moved toward "making people feel things… less about retention data analytics" ([Colin and Samir, "MrBeast Reflects on the State of YouTube", xf0Mli0LVgI 49:36–49:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xf0Mli0LVgI&t=2976) [tx]).
- **Full-video A/B scope.** The ImqahdmBXo8 description and Hanif (j7GtybYc6gk 6:56) say "for Shorts" / "starting off with Shorts"; the ImqahdmBXo8 narration (3:15–3:29) says "upload your cuts, short or long form". Unresolved.
- **Too much data stops learning** (Galloway, j7GtybYc6gk 16:29–16:38; Hanif: "a lot to interpret", 13:59–14:05). **Copying outliers** becomes "constantly chasing what's worked for other people" (17:45–17:53). **Analytics describe the past**: "very good at telling you what happened", hard at predicting (12:22–12:31). All PRACTITIONER [T1]/[T3], [tx].
- **One channel's correlations don't transfer** (Hoyos: "at least for my channel", D5.5).
