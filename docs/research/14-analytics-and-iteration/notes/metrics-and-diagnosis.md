### Negative and contested (item 1)
- **No published cross-channel benchmark for retention or APV.** YouTube publishes only own-channel bands (last 10 similar-length videos), the per-video "all YouTube videos of similar length" comparison, the "≥50% at 0:30" card threshold, and the 2–10% CTR distribution. Blog figures such as "average retention 23.7%" (Retention Rabbit, repeated by SEO blogs) have no methodology page (via note 07). **Do not use them.**
- **Spike = good moment?** Contested by YouTube itself: a spike can mean content that was "unclear" and replayed (Help 1715160), skipping forward (V8f_VWMV8pY 00:49), or chapter navigation (zNy3jxmT26o 03:03).
- **Views and engaged views changed meaning in 2026.** Since 2026-08-24 (Help 2991785) hover-autoplay counts as a view. vidIQ dates the change 2026-08-27 (We8TVsjs2B8 00:33), so the two sources conflict. Views per video before and after the change are **not comparable**, and the denominator under the Studio retention curve after the change is not documented (see Gaps).
- **"Watch time is king"** is retracted by YouTube's own statements. Time is weighted by surveys (Goodrow 2021), and relative and absolute time are read separately (Beaupré 2026, 1.8).
- **A subscription is a weak signal.** Subscribers click about 10% or less of uploads in their own feed (rHLjxrbXmmY 37:03–38:08). Subscriber count is not a proxy for audience (Help 141805; 10246996).
- **AVP vs AVD.** The MrBeast memo downplays AVP for production staff. Beaupré says look at both relative (percent) and absolute time. Film Booth calls the absolute APV number meaningless outside your own baseline. These positions do not conflict on the practical rule, which is to compare within length bands on your own channel.

### 2c. Negative and contested (item 2)
- **Early-drop interpretation after the 2026 view change.** vidIQ reads the 54% engaged-view ratio as "the hook failed almost half of the time" ([We8TVsjs2B8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=We8TVsjs2B8&t=677) at 11:17–11:40). A hover-play that never commits is closer to an impression than to a rejected hook. Until YouTube documents whether the retention curve uses all views or engaged views, the first ~10 s of a post-2026-08-24 curve should **not** be compared with older curves.
- **Turning off subscriber notifications or embeds "to protect metrics"** is contested by YouTube: Beaupré "wouldn't advise" turning notifications off, and he says traffic sources that don't predict Home behaviour carry little weight (2.9). Some creators claim it helps; no data is shown either way.
- **"A dud tanks the channel" / penalty box.** Denied at the algorithm level (1.19, [wrIlifWnKDs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wrIlifWnKDs&t=366) at 06:06–06:35). It remains true through audience memory: a dud can hurt the next video's CTR.
- **Deleting viral off-niche videos** to "fix" the audience: Beaupré "wouldn't advise deleting videos" ([wrIlifWnKDs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wrIlifWnKDs&t=96) at 01:36–02:02).
- **Suggested-traffic lists as proof that "the algorithm misunderstands my audience".** Wrong reading: watch-page suggestions are personalised viewer interests, not topical relatedness ([rHLjxrbXmmY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY&t=1738) at 28:58–30:49).
- **First-hour CTR as a predictor.** Galloway claims a strong correlation on established channels (2.3). YouTube says early data is not decisive (2.7). Both can hold: early CTR may predict, while early *disappointment* is often reversed. No public dataset tests this.
- **The curve shape → cause pairings (D6–D12)** rest on n=1 practitioner readings, mostly Blackman 2022–23 (Old, small and mid channels). They are diagnostic hypotheses to test, not established rules.
