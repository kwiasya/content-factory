# 07 · Notes: keeping the middle (item 3) and pacing and editing tempo (item 4)

Researcher share: items 3 and 4 of `prompts/07-retention-long-form.md`. Collected 2026-10-12.

## Method and caveats

- **Search budget.** The shared WebSearch limit for this run ran out after 8 of my queries, short of the 15 the brief asked for. To make up for it I fetched primary sources directly: YouTube Help pages, the YouTube blog, the leaked MrBeast production document, and paper metadata and abstracts via Crossref and Semantic Scholar by DOI. I also read YouTube metadata and caption tracks with `yt-dlp`. I ran no search through those tools; video IDs came from channel upload lists.
- **Transcript timestamps** come from each video's English caption track (json3), fetched 2026-10-12. "subs" marks the uploader's captions, "auto" marks YouTube's auto-captions.
- **Chapter timestamps** come from the chapter list in the video's YouTube metadata. YouTube turns automatic chapters on by default ([YouTube Help, chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)). The descriptive chapter titles on MrBeast videos are probably auto-generated. Veritasium and Johnny Harris chapters read as creator-written.
- **"Most replayed" heatmap** is YouTube's public replay-intensity graph from the player: 100 bins normalised to 0–1, read here from the `heatmap` field in `yt-dlp`. **It is not audience retention.** A trough close to 0 followed straight away by a spike is the signature of viewers seeking past a section. Each heatmap reading is one video: a measurement, but n = 1. I tag these "DATA (n=1, heatmap)" and do not treat them as a sample.
- **Tags** follow the CONTEXT block. YouTube staff statements are tier 1 but show no data, so they are tagged PRACTITIONER, and the tier is noted next to the tag.

### 3b. Negative and contested

| Claim | Who says what | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Open loops work because of the Zeigarnik effect: people remember unfinished things" | A 2025 meta-analysis (screened from 1,455 publications) found "**no memory advantage for unfinished tasks**" but "a general tendency to resume tasks" (the Ovsiankina effect). The Zeigarnik effect "lacks universal validity". [Ghibellini & Meier, *Humanities & Social Sciences Communications* 2025-07-01, doi:10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w) [verified: Crossref abstract quotes both phrases; the article reports 1455 publications from the first search, 1349 after duplicates; published 2025-07-01] | **THEORY, against the folk explanation.** Base open loops on the information gap and on resumption (3.6, 3.7), not on memory. |
| "Never reveal the outcome early, or tension dies" | Leavitt & Christenfeld, "Story Spoilers Don't Spoil Stories", *Psychological Science* 2011, [doi:10.1177/0956797611417007](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611417007): the title states the finding; abstract not retrieved. The MrBeast guide (3.5) holds the winner back to the end | **Contested.** Short-story lab results against creator practice for contests. A flash-forward that shows *what* happens may keep tension that rests on *how*. Not tested on video. |
| A teaser before an ad keeps viewers through the ad | Johnny Harris heatmap (3.8): near-zero intensity during both sponsor reads, spike at re-entry | **Against, n=1.** The tease protects the session; viewers still skip the ad. |
| Fixed re-hooks at 3 and 6 minutes are universal | The guide's marks fit its own average: "the average mrbeast video is 818 seconds or 13 minutes and 37 seconds" | **Unvalidated** for 20–30 min essays. No source scales the marks. |
| More pattern interrupts = more attention | Lang: scene cuts above ~10 per 2 min reduced recognition; unrelated cuts are not encoded; (3.14, 3.15); the overload part is unconfirmed, see "Refuted or unconfirmed" | **Backfire condition (THEORY).** Interrupts must relate to the story. |
| Fake open loops and artificial stretching raise watch time and reach | YouTube discounts unvalued time (3.17, 3.18). Neal Mohan: YouTube is "building on our established systems… combatting spam and clickbait" ([2026 letter, 2026-01-21](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026/)) | **Against (tier 1, no data shown).** A loop that is not paid off costs the satisfaction signals. |
| The back half can be weaker | The MrBeast guide says so, but adds: "Many videos have been killed because the back half content was ass". Mark Rober's game 2 shows a mid-video skip (3.10) | **Nuanced.** "Weaker" means slower and more explanatory, not filler. |

## Gaps

1. **Search budget:** only 8 web searches ran before the shared limit. I did not reach tier 3 analysts directly: Paddy Galloway's newsletter, Film Booth, Colin and Samir, vidIQ studies. paddygalloway.com has no public articles. I found no sample-level analyst data on re-hooks, open loops or cut rate.
2. **No creator retention graphs for middle techniques.** I found no published A/B test or retention-graph comparison for open loops, "but first", chapter promises, music changes or b-roll rate. The heatmap readings (3.8, 3.10, 3.11, 3.12) are single-video replay data, not retention.
3. **Music changes:** nothing sourced. Caption music tags were inconsistent across years: the 2022 MrBeast captions tag music changes, the 2023–24 captions tag none. They cannot serve as a proxy.
4. **Cuts per minute on YouTube:** no measured dataset. Measuring it needs shot detection on downloaded video, which I did not do without permission. Film data (Cutting) and lab data (Lang) do not transfer cleanly to talking-head jump cuts.
5. **Primary texts not retrieved:** Lang et al. 1999 abstract (403), Loewenstein 1994 (scanned PDF), Leavitt & Christenfeld 2011 abstract (publisher elides it). Their findings rest on titles and secondary summaries. I did not find replications that might contest "spoilers don't spoil".
6. **Beaupré 2026 quotes** are secondhand via PPC Land. The Creator Insider transcript could not be fetched (YouTube bot wall), and PPC Land says some audio tracks had garbled speaker names. Verify the exact second against the video.
7. **MrBeast guide:** undated, roughly 2023–24. Its 3- and 6-minute marks fit 13–14-minute videos; I found no source for 20–30-minute essays. The title link "I Bought The World's Largest Firework" = erQ_9yEz0ls is my inference.
8. **The 2024–26 "slow era":** MrBeast's "views have skyrocketed" has no figures. I found no YouTube data showing that slower videos retain better. YouTube's line is about length and TV demand, not cutting tempo.
9. **Genre differences for YouTube pacing** (essay, vlog, documentary, challenge) have no sample data. Only MOOC lecture versus tutorial data (2014) and practitioner statements exist.
10. **Chapters' effect on retention:** YouTube Help is silent; no tier 1–4 data found.
