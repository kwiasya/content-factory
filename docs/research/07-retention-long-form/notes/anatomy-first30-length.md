Researcher share of prompt `docs/research/prompts/07-retention-long-form.md`, items 1, 2 and 6. Collected 2026-10-12.

Tags as the prompt defines them: **DATA** (measurement over a sample, an A/B test or a shown graph), **PRACTITIONER** (stated practice, no data shown), **THEORY** (research or model not tested on YouTube video). Tier in brackets: T1 YouTube/Google, T2 creator showing own data, T3 analyst from samples, T4 peer-reviewed.

**Where timestamps come from.** Every YouTube timestamp below comes from that video's own YouTube caption track. I fetched it with `yt-dlp --print automatic_captions` and grouped the lines into blocks of about 8 caption events. A timestamp therefore marks the start of a block of 10 to 20 seconds, not the exact word. Chapter lists come from the same metadata. Where a breakdown video describes another video, the timestamp is in the breakdown, and I say so.

### 1c. Anatomy as a sequence (synthesis of the rows above, each step sourced)

1. **0–~10 s:** a share of counted views is impressions that started playing and never committed. Since 2026-08-24 these count as views (1.11). vidIQ measures only ~54% of views as engaged (1.12), so the first seconds of a curve built on all views now fall more steeply than before the change. Whether the Studio retention graph is built on views or engaged views is **not stated** anywhere I found; see Gaps.
2. **0–30 s:** YouTube's own "Intro" checkpoint. ≥50% still watching is "above typical" (1.2, 1.3). MrBeast's ~65% at 1:00 was "higher than average" (1.14).
3. **Middle:** a gradual decline is normal (1.4, 1.8). Spikes mark rewatched, shared or demo moments (1.8, 1.9). The MrBeast memo says that after the half-way point most viewers stay (1.16).
4. **End:** a drop when the outro is signposted (1.8). Item 7 covers this in depth.

## Gaps

1. **Platform-wide retention benchmarks by genre (essay, vlog, tutorial, challenge, documentary)** at 30 s, AVD and APV: I found **no tier 1–3 source with a disclosed sample**. YouTube publishes none, and Studio benchmarks only against your own similar-length videos. The widely cited Retention Rabbit 2025 numbers have no findable primary report. The only genre split with data is Guo 2014 (lectures vs tutorials, MOOC, not YouTube).
2. **Is the Studio retention graph (and the Intro %) computed on all views or on engaged views after the 2026-08-24 change?** Not stated in Help 2991785. This decides whether 30-second benchmarks from before and after Aug 2026 are comparable. The official Creator Insider explainer "New! Updates to Public View Counts & Engaged Views Explained" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HI03HqWdX0k) could not be read: YouTube bot-blocked yt-dlp, and WebFetch returns no page body. The effective date also conflicts: Help says 2026-08-24, vidIQ says 2026-08-27.
3. **Controlled data on cold open vs branded intro**: none found; all evidence is PRACTITIONER. YouTube has no intro A/B tool.
4. **Separating packaging mismatch from a slow opening**: YouTube gives the levers (new vs returning, traffic source, subscriptions feed) but **no published method or thresholds**. Rule 2.17 is my inference from Beaupré's statement, not a YouTube rule.
5. **Paddy Galloway's own channel and newsletter**: the @paddygalloway handle returned 404 in yt-dlp, and paddygalloway.com has no articles. His data here is only what he showed on Colin and Samir (sample sizes not stated).
6. **YouTube Creators "Help Your YouTube Videos Stand Out & Keep Viewers Watching"** (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6NKhcV3v2nw) could not be read for the same bot-block reason.
7. **The 51-minute woodworking example (2.11)**: the creator and link are not named in the transcript, and with search used up I could not identify the video.
8. **Red Bull Everest video (6.18)**: no direct link; it is cited only through Galloway's description.
9. **vidIQ AVD blog (1.18, 1.19)**: the page returned 403, so its figures come from a search-engine extract. I could not check them against the page.
10. **"Regret" wording**: the Goodrow 2021 blog quote reached me only through outlierkit's verbatim citation, because my direct fetch of the blog did not surface that line. Beaupré's 2026 "hung on … hoping for a payoff" statement is verified from the transcript.
11. **Conflicting length readings** (Galloway's longer and slower vs Colin's 6–8 min on TV vs YouTube's run-time matches content): no causal or controlled data separates the effect of length from the effect of topic or concept.
12. Web search budget ran out after 14 queries (the target was 15). Coverage of 1of10, Jon Youshaei, and 2025–2026 Film Booth retention case studies is therefore thin.
