# 07 · Notes: items 5 (diagnosis), 7 (endings), 8 (negative findings)

Researcher share of prompt `docs/research/prompts/07-retention-long-form.md`. Written 2026-10-12.

How to read the tags:
- **DATA**: a measurement over a sample, an A/B test, or a retention graph is shown.
- **PRACTITIONER**: a creator's or analyst's stated practice with no data shown. When an analyst reads a shown graph, the curve shape counts as DATA (n=1), but the cause they name is PRACTITIONER.
- **THEORY**: research or a model that was not tested on video.

**Tier** refers to the source hierarchy in the CONTEXT block. **Old** means the source is from before 2024 and may be outdated.

Source caveats:
- **George Blackman's "Retention Review" (RR) series.** Blackman, a YouTube scriptwriter, read client retention graphs in his newsletter Write On Time, Nov 2022 to Mar 2023. It is tier 2/3 and Old. The graphs are images in the posts. Every timestamp below that comes from an RR post is the reviewer's own, taken from that published breakdown.
- **"How To Succeed In MrBeast Production".** This is the internal MrBeast onboarding document. I read a copy rendered by Google Docs: the PDF producer string is "Skia/PDF m129", which matches the Chrome build of Sept 2024. It circulated publicly in Sept 2024. ### Negative and contested (item 5)
- **Spike means "good moment"?** Contested. YouTube itself says a spike may mean unclear content was replayed (5.3). Practitioners show spikes caused by skipping a hook (5.14) or skipping to a tip (5.18). A spike should be read together with the dip that follows it.
- **The "relative retention" vs "typical retention" naming.** Third-party guides still describe a "relative retention" curve in Studio. YouTube's current Help does not use the term; only the API keeps `relativeRetentionPerformance`. The typical-retention baseline also changed definition (5.7 vs 5.8). Old tutorials are unreliable on this.
- **Slow intros.** RR#3's Only Trails held 80% at 0:30 despite a slow start (5.21). This contradicts the general rule "cut to content fast", but only for a loyal niche audience. In RR#5, Breakout Cards found the opposite: cutting straight to the first deal *worsened* early retention on the creator's other videos. Both are n=1.

### Negative and contested (item 7)
- **An abrupt end vs a next-video hand-off.** MrBeast says always end abruptly (7.3). Blackman criticises an abrupt end without a next-video pointer (7.13) and wants the outro to be a CTA (7.9, 7.12). The two agree on no recap, no signalling and no lingering. They differ on whether the closing seconds should carry a spoken hand-off. Neither side shows a graph that compares the two endings.
- **An abrupt ending does not abolish the end cliff** (7.4). A 20–30 point drop in the final seconds appears even on MrBeast videos. An agent should not flag a final-seconds drop as a defect by itself. A drop that starts 30–120 s before the end, in an outro, recap or caveat, is the actionable one (7.8, 7.10, 7.11).
- **Reflective outros.** Blackman himself says they can work in some niches (7.10), which reverses his usual "finish and get out" advice.
- **End-screen impact on retention or reach.** YouTube publishes requirements and click-rate metrics only. There is no first-party statement that end screens or "session time" change recommendations. The session-time claim in RR#4 is unsourced.

### Negative and contested (item 8)
- **Is clickbait bad?** Contested at the margin. Veritasium's ["Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2xHZPH5Sng) (title and channel confirmed via oEmbed; I could not get the transcript, date or timestamps) argues for bold packaging. The MB doc openly says "match the expectations of the clickbait" (5.12). The consistent line across tier 1–3 is that a strong promise is fine **if the opening delivers it**. Mismatch is what fails: by YouTube's description of its own 2016 recommender, ranking moved off clicks to expected watch time per impression (8.1); the malicious-clickbait policy applies (8.6); and the early dips in 8.8 follow.
- **"Watch time is king."** YouTube itself retracted this as the sole target: watch time was added in 2012 (8.2), then weighted by survey-rated "valued watch time" (8.4), and in 2025 framed as satisfaction and long-run return (8.5).
- **Typical-retention baseline.** The definition changed between 2021 (channel videos from the past year) and now (the 10 latest similar-length videos): 5.7 vs 5.8.
- **Slower videos and MrBeast's own reversal.** Secondary sources claim MrBeast backed away from hyper-fast cutting in 2024–2025. I could not open a primary source (see Gaps). The 2024 MB doc itself still asks for "lots of quick scene changes" in minutes 3–6.
