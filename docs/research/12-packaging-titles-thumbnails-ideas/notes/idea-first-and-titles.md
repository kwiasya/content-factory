### 1c. Negative and contested

- **Outlier copying is contested.** Galloway (2026) says outliers are inputs to a twist, never something to repeat (1.5). 1of10 says keep the format and change the subject, and treats 50 copies a month as saturated (1.15). vidIQ (2024) titled a video "Why Your Favorite YouTuber is Stealing Ideas" ([i2R_jhzEl4I](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2R_jhzEl4I), 2024-03-15; transcript not read, rate-limited). MrBeast publicly objected to clone channels, as framed by vidIQ ([mF_yu5CdE3I](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mF_yu5CdE3I), 2023-07-07; not read). PRACT only.
- **Format repetition decays.** MrBeast: "if you do the same stuff over and over again you'll eventually fall off" ([21:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9IQ_ldV9z_A&t=1277)). PRACT.
- **Outlier thresholds disagree**: Galloway "3 or 4x average"; 1of10 "several times median", no number; OverseerOS needs ≥5x for its "strong" breakouts ([OverseerOS, 2026-08-16](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/best-youtube-title-length)). Average and median also differ.
- **Survivorship bias.** Galloway names it himself (1.12). The 1of10 and vidIQ multipliers are computed on winners with no denominator of failed copies.
- **Platform statement against "dud penalty" folklore.** Todd Beaupré (YouTube Growth & Discovery): "we aim not to hold back videos based on other videos performance". However, "the audience does remember", so a bad video hurts the next one's CTR through audience reaction, not through the algorithm ([3:19–4:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HP-rqigfw2s&t=199)). Tier 1, PRACT. It supports scoring each idea on its own merits while protecting trust. [verified]
- **The leaked "extreme" advice ("the more extreme the better")** sits next to "APPLY WITH A GRAIN OF SALT" on simplicity in the same document. The guide is written for a $-budget challenge channel, and no source tests whether it transfers to POV walks or talking-head videos.

### 2e. Negative and contested

- **CTR is not the target metric for YouTube's own test tool.** Test & Compare ranks by watch time per impression (2.7, 2.8). A title that wins on CTR in a third-party tool can lose on YouTube (2.9). Creators keep asking for CTR, and YouTube declined in 2024 and again in 2026.
- **Title length is contested to the point of noise.** Within-channel analyses find ~0 effect (2.20, 2.21). Pooled cross-channel studies give "rules" from 20 to 70 characters that contradict each other (2.23). The only peer-reviewed study is Japanese-only and niche-only (2.22). The defensible rule is the platform's: put the key words first, because titles truncate (2.5). Galloway's ≤50 characters is a practitioner heuristic.
- **Question titles: two vendor datasets point negative** (−0.4%, 0.82x). That contradicts older advice that questions drive curiosity. No tier-1/2 test was found.
- **Emotional and negative words:** the only rigorous evidence is Upworthy news headlines (2.24). Joy and positive words *lowered* clicks. Veritasium's "Surprising" removal (+10%) points the same way for hype adjectives. Nothing shows the effect transfers to YouTube titles or to retention.
- **Emoji splits by format**: positive on Shorts and negative on average for long-form reach (vidIQ). YTGrowth finds 0 for long-form. Both are correlational.
- **Saturated in 2026 per vidIQ**: question endings, "I was wrong" confessions, "Everything you need to know about X is wrong". vidIQ's method (how "lift" is computed, channel normalization, CTR vs views) is not published, so treat it as directional.
- **Pattern multipliers are survivor statistics.** vidIQ 2026 and 1of10 2024 report how often a pattern shows up in winners, not P(win | pattern). vidIQ's own caveat is "content amplifiers, not content fixers" (1.22).
- **Retitle wins can't be separated from the thumbnail.** Veritasium's asteroid case changed both, and YouTube says there is no re-index boost (2.11), so a jump is audience response. Without an A/B test, timing effects (audience mix shifting over time, 2.12) confound before/after comparisons.
- **Trend reversal.** Hanif and Galloway (2026): a winning look (orange jumpsuit, mouth open) stops standing out once everyone copies it ([5:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7GtybYc6gk&t=341); [31:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HP-rqigfw2s&t=1872)). Title patterns plausibly decay the same way (vidIQ's "burned out" list), but no source measures how fast.

## Gaps

1. **No measurement that the idea dominates execution.** Every "idea > everything" statement (Galloway, Chucky, vidIQ) is PRACT. The one concrete counter-case (MrBeast hotel → yacht, same format, B → A++) is also PRACT. A 1of10 podcast reportedly covers "300,000+ outliers" and ideal title length ([podscan listing](https://podscan.fm/e/ep_b54prplbn957je6l)), but no transcript or numbers were found.
2. **No published creator title A/B result from Test & Compare (2025–2026) with numbers.** The thumbnail A/B evidence (Chucky's 30/30 closed-mouth wins, [22:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HP-rqigfw2s&t=1327)) is thumbnail-only. The title-specific creator data I found is Veritasium 2021 (pre-Test & Compare, before/after, title and thumbnail changed together).
3. **CTR benchmarks by traffic source or format**: YouTube publishes only the 2–10% "half of channels" range. It gives no Shorts CTR, no per-surface benchmark, and no update date on the help page.
4. **Vendor data quality**: vidIQ (4M titles, 128M emoji), YTGrowth, OverseerOS and 1of10 do not publish full methods or datasets. vidIQ's "lift" baseline is unclear (channel average vs median). 1of10's outlier threshold is undisclosed. All are correlational, with views as the outcome rather than CTR.
5. **Banerjee & Urminsky (Upworthy headline-language study)** could not be opened (SSRN 403). That study likely holds the concreteness and specificity results this item asks for.
6. **The Galloway #152 and #209 timestamps** inside chapters come from the podcast page transcript, not from audio. Chapter starts are reliable.
7. **The MrBeast 2025 "show sucks" quote** has an unclear speaker in the third-party transcript. YouTube rate-limited caption reads for that video, so it was not verified against the captions. Paddy Galloway's original X threads (CCN, "5 traits", the 2025 "1.3k-sub channel" strategy) returned HTTP 402 and are cited only through the podcasts.
8. **Short-form titles**: the only data is vidIQ's emoji split and OverseerOS's Shorts length (median 49 chars). Test & Compare excludes Shorts. No Shorts title-test data exists from YouTube, TikTok or Meta in my sources (item 4 covers short-form).
9. **Russian- and other non-English-language title data**: none found beyond the Japanese peer-reviewed study.
10. The WebSearch budget for this run ran out after 7 queries (shared limit). The rest of the research used direct fetches of known pages and channel listings plus YouTube caption tracks. YouTube rate-limited caption reads near the end, so vidIQ's "Why Your Favorite YouTuber is Stealing Ideas" and "How YouTube Geniuses Find Ideas", the Made on YouTube 2026 recap and the Colin and Samir 2025 MrBeast interview are cited from metadata only or not at all.
