### 5. Negative and contested

- **No public measurement of "clickbait debt" on YouTube.** YouTube says the cost runs through audience memory and later CTR (5.4) and through survey-weighted satisfaction (5.2, 5.3), but it publishes no effect size for returning viewers or subscriber churn after a misleading package. The only platform-scale number is the 20% view drop when watch time replaced clicks (5.2, 2012 event, **[old]**). Creators cannot see satisfaction data (5.19).
- **The research on trust conflicts.** 5.23 (credibility may fall) and 5.24 (no effect on trust) disagree. Both are one-shot news-headline experiments with no repeated exposure, which is the condition clickbait debt describes. 5.22 favours plain headlines for news, but it measures news selection, not entertainment video.
- **Clickbait features reliably buy clicks** (5.20, 5.21), so a scorer that only penalizes them will lose CTR. YouTube's watch-time-based testing (5.5) is the arbiter that prices both sides.
- **Alignment is not one-to-one.** MrBeast does not always make the intro mirror the thumbnail (5.12). Galloway accepts exaggerated framing of content that is really there (5.30 example). Veritasium calls the boundary "fuzzy" (5.25). The bright line that policy and practice share is narrower: the **object or claim shown in the package must exist in the video and show up early**. Withheld outcomes are allowed. A missing subject is not (5.9, 5.16, 5.26–5.29).
- **Enforcement scope.** Egregious-clickbait removal was announced for India, news first (5.28). A 1of10 post dated 2026 repeats the policy "as of April 2026" but cites nothing new ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/clickbait-thumbnail/), 2026-05-01). No expansion to other markets was confirmed. ### 6. Negative and contested

- **The test optimizes watch time, not truthfulness.** A misleading variant can still win if viewers stay. Policy limits (5.26–5.29) apply on top of any test result.
- **Winners are not stable.** They depend on the audience and the moment (6.18). A single test result is local evidence, not a rule for future videos.
- **Pre-publish polls may be biased toward the core audience.** This is an inference, not a measured result. Patreon and Twitter followers (6.10) are core fans, and Beaupré says new viewers react differently, with faces helping familiar viewers more (7.6). No source compares pre-publish poll winners with Test & Compare winners.
- **Shorts cannot use Test & Compare** (6.14). Video A/B testing for Shorts or long form is announced but not shipped (6.16). For Reels and TikTok, no native package test was sourced (see Gaps).
- **Creators want CTR in test results, but YouTube withholds it deliberately** (5.5, 5.6). Galloway agrees that CTR "tends to go down" as a video goes viral ([j7GtybYc6gk 12:09–12:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j7GtybYc6gk&t=729s)).
