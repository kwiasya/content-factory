# C: Idea-Scoring Rubric

For each new video concept, score these criteria (pass = ✔):

- **Novelty/Value (Originality)** – Is the core idea fresh and big enough to stand out?  *High score if “never been done”, “amazing concept people *need* to see” (MrBeast emphasis); low if mundane.  *(Pass:* ✓ “X never done before” or extreme twist.)*
- **Curiosity/Emotional Hook** – Does the title/thumbnail promise a compelling payoff?  *High if it provokes strong emotion or curiosity (“irresistible hook”, extreme claim) and “you’ll be annoyed if you don’t click”. Low if bland.*  *(Pass:* ✓ clear question or bold promise that screams “click me” without being false.)*
- **Clarity & Promise Alignment** – Is the title/hook believable and backed by content?  *High if title “leads into content and represents length”. Avoid vague hyperbole that can’t be delivered. *(Pass:* ✓ Title *exactly* matches what video delivers.)*
- **Audience Demand/Interest** – Does keyword research/Google Trends/competitor data show strong interest?  *High if tools (Google Trends for YouTube, TubeBuddy, etc.) show rising demand or if niche outliers are high-view topics. *(Pass:* ✓ Idea targets known trending interest or fills a demand gap.)*
- **Feasibility/Cost** – Can we actually produce it?  *First brainstorm “wild” ideas, then filter by budget/logic. Score high only if a practical plan exists (find alternatives if needed, per MrBeast's island example). *(Pass:* ✓ Idea can be done within time/budget.)*
- **Return on Effort** – Is expected reward (views, brand lift) >> effort?  *High if idea could yield substantial ROI (outlier potential). Low if mediocre. *(Pass:* ✓ Outlier-caliber content, or clearly broaden audience.)*

*>Evidence:* MrBeast stresses *idea quality first* over execution. In practice, creators brainstorm many ideas and then weed out “not doable” ones. The strongest ideas have a bold, crystal-clear promise (high curiosity) that the execution can fulfill.*

# D: A/B Testing Protocol (small channel)

- **Testable Items:** Titles and thumbnail variants (YouTube’s Test & Compare allows up to 3 combos). For Shorts/Reels, use Title/cover text in content as analog.
- **Procedure:** Use YouTube Studio’s **A/B test** (if enabled) or third-party tools. Test *one change at a time* (title vs thumbnail). For small channels without YouTube’s feature, alternate releases with different thumbnails or ask subscribers via community polls.
- **Duration & Sample:** Run for ~2 weeks to accumulate impressions. Target at least hundreds of views per variant; more is better. (YouTube notes tests with too few impressions often end “inconclusive”.)
- **Reading Results:** YouTube chooses the variant with highest *watch time* share as winner. If “performed the same” or “inconclusive”, difference is negligible. For a small channel, expect low statistical confidence: look for clear CTR/watch-time gaps (~2×) before trusting a lead.
- **Tips:** Test older videos first to avoid harming new content. Make options clearly different (avoid minor tweaks). After testing, switch to winning title/thumbnail.

# E: Anti-Patterns and Policy Limits

- **False/Misleading Promises:** Thumbnails or titles that don’t match video content *harm retention and trust*. YouTube explicitly flags “high CTR, low watch time” as clickbait. Overpromising (e.g. “5-Minute Life Hacks!” with no payoff) creates “clickbait debt” (viewer annoyance and fewer returners).
- **“Clickbait” Tropes (avoid):** Big red arrows, circles, “shocked” emoji faces, exaggerated claims (“UNBELIEVABLE!”) are widely seen as spammy. Community consensus is to avoid them – they can trigger viewer backlash (reports or dislike). While not banned per se, YouTube’s algorithms *demote* videos with poor view-duration (clickbait signs).
- **Misleading Thumbnail Policy:** YouTube’s guidelines prohibit content that misleads viewers. If your thumbnail lure isn’t fulfilled by video content, you risk strikes or demonetization. (Example: a face overlay with mysterious text that isn’t addressed in video could be deemed misleading.)
- **Overused Formats:** In 2025–26, certain styles are saturated. For instance, shock-faced selfies or super-saturated backgrounds were ubiquitous in 2020s; freshness now favors cleaner, unique branding. Repeating meme-style thumbnails risks blending into noise.
- **Platform Policies:** YouTube’s evolving rules emphasize authenticity. As of 2025, any “inauthentic” tactics (over-processed AI imagery, irrelevant stock photos, etc.) can undermine monetization status. In short, strong but honest packaging that delivers on its promise is required.

*>Evidence:* YouTube’s data show clickbait decreases recommendations (high CTR+low retention). Community and creator forums consistently call out arrows/circles/shock-faces as misleading tactics. The safe line is: make big promises you can fulfill; otherwise algorithms and viewers will punish the content.
