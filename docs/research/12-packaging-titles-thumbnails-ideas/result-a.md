# 12 · Packaging and ideas: titles, thumbnails, idea validation

Source: Claude Opus research agents (3 researchers, 3 fact-checkers, 1 writer), 2026-10-12. Notes: notes/.

## How to read this file

- **Tags.** **DATA**: a measurement over a sample, an A/B test or a retention graph is shown. **PRACTITIONER**: a stated practice with no data shown. **THEORY**: research or a model not tested on video. YouTube Help pages and YouTube staff statements describe the platform and show no performance data, so they are PRACTITIONER and marked **T1**. A creator who states test numbers without showing them is PRACTITIONER (numbers stated). One video's view count is never DATA.
- **Vendor DATA** (vidIQ, YTGrowth, OverseerOS, 1of10) counts as tier 3. These vendors sell tools and do not publish their full methods. Their pattern multipliers show how often a pattern appears among winners, not the probability that it wins. vidIQ's own caveat is that patterns are "content amplifiers, not content fixers" ([vidIQ 3:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n5NER9cbueY&t=181)).
- **[old]** means before 2024 and possibly outdated.
- **Timestamps** come from YouTube auto-caption tracks, so start about 15 s before the cited time. "(page)" means the timestamp comes from a podcast page's transcript, not from the audio.

### A2. Title length: the sources conflict

| Position | Who says it | Evidence | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 50 characters | Galloway: "Most of the best titles I've put together… are usually 50 characters or under" (sometimes up to ~60) | Creator Science #152, ~8:46 (page) **[old]** | PRACTITIONER |
| Length has no effect within a channel | YTGrowth: r = 0.0046. The worst decile (1–35 characters) ran −2% and the best (91–97) +1% vs channel norm. The null holds in every niche (the page says 15 in its headline finding and 14 later). The median title is 66 characters / 11 words. | YTGrowth | DATA (vendor) |
| Breakouts are not shorter than the channel's usual titles | OverseerOS, 942 breakouts at ≥5x baseline from 137 channels. Median 55 characters (long-form 62, Shorts 49). Channels with 1M+ subscribers run shorter (long-form 53, Shorts 40). The median ratio to the channel's own norm is 1.00x, and 62% fall within ±20%. The authors say this "does not equal optimum". | [OverseerOS, 2026-08-16](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/best-youtube-title-length) | DATA (vendor, winner-only sample) |
| Bell curve peaking at 40–49 **Japanese** characters | 12,466 Japanese Excel-tutorial videos (2010–2023), Kruskal–Wallis p < 0.001. Character count matters more than word count. One language and one niche; thumbnails not controlled. | Kawata, Kato & Tsuda, *EAI Endorsed Trans. Scalable Inf. Syst.* 12(1), published 2024-12-16, [doi:10.4108/eetsis.7779](https://eudl.eu/doi/10.4108/eetsis.7779) | DATA (tier 4) |
| Pooled studies give rules from 20 to 70 characters that contradict each other | Briggsby 20–40 characters (100k videos). ViewsKit: under 30 characters ≈ 2x the views of 80–100 characters (61,838 videos). Subsub 65–70 characters (120,703 videos). YTGrowth argues that pooling across channels creates spurious rules. | YTGrowth's summary; the original studies were not opened | DATA (vendor, secondary) |

**What the agent can defend:** put the key words first, because titles get truncated (A1, T1). Treat ≤ 50 characters as a practitioner heuristic, not a measured optimum.

## Table B · Thumbnail rules × evidence × example

### B1. Composition rules

| # | Rule | Evidence | Tag | Example (named video, link, timestamp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Short-form verdicts from the notes.** For reach, the first frame is the thumbnail. For the channel page, search and sponsors, upload a clean 9:16 cover that matches frame 1, and spend minutes on it, not hours. On Shorts, write a short, literal title; on TikTok and Instagram, put keywords in the caption. The on-screen hook text does the packaging job: a short label over a real visual, not a text card (Instagram demotes "majority text"). The 5–10 words/s figure applies to ads and speech-synced captions, not to cover text.

**No measured clickbait debt exists for any video platform:** no published effect on returning viewers, subscriber churn or next-video CTR. The only platform-scale number is the 20% drop in views when watch time replaced clicks (2012). Because clickbait features reliably buy clicks (5.20, 5.21), a scorer that only penalizes them will lose CTR. YouTube's watch-time test is the arbiter that prices both sides.
