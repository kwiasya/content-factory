### 1.C Negative and contested (item 1)

- **Structure adherence ≠ success.** Boyd et al. 2020 found no link between following the normative arc and popularity (DATA, text). Reagan 2016 links arc shape to downloads only weakly (proxy). Treat any framework as a *checklist for causality and escalation*, not a success predictor.
- **Fiction arcs do not transfer one-to-one to explainers.** TED and NYT science texts lack the mid-story tension peak (Boyd 2020). Explainers carry tension as open questions (curiosity), not character conflict.
- **"Every minute better than the last"** has no primary source. The memo describes a lull in the back half, with weaker material placed there on purpose.
- **The minute plan is dated.** It was written around about 10-min videos ("Let's say we have 10 minute video…"). MrBeast's 2025–26 uploads run 19–58 min (channel listing, 2026-10-12), and he publicly retired the "ultra fast paced/overstim" style in March 2024. Use the *ratios* (front-load, a re-engagement every about 3 min, payoff last), not the absolute minutes.
- **Misconception-first can backfire on accuracy.** Veritasium's electricity video drew expert pushback and a 24-min follow-up, [*How Electricity Actually Works*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oI_X2cMHNe0) (2022-04-29). The video itself admits at 12:35 (transcript): "I asked several experts … got kind of different answers". A misconception set-up that oversimplifies the "right" answer invites correction videos.
- **Muller's DATA measures learning, not retention.** Students rated the better-learning videos as *more confusing* and *more effortful* (THE Journal summary). On a feed, that effort may cost watch time. No tier 1–3 source tests this.
- **Hero's journey:** no creator-side data. The cited 2023 JPSP work is about life stories and meaning. The MrBeast boat video shows the circle can end in failure (Take without Return) and still be the format. One video's views are not evidence.

### 2.C Negative and contested (item 2)

- **Fake stakes:** no tier 1–3 source quantifies the cost of overstated stakes. The closest are the memo's "match the expectations of the clickbait" and YouTube's own note that the 30-second "Intro" metric shows whether the opening matched the title and thumbnail ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)). The Veritasium spider-silk team jokes about it on screen (24:00): "used the title 'Risking my life…' too early".
- **Intensity ≠ stakes:** MrBeast's 2024 reversal away from fast cuts and screaming (self-report; Tubefilter notes views have many causes).
- **Negativity's evidence is for clicks** (Robertson 2023), and sad language *reduces* continued reading (Berger, Moe & Schweidel 2023). Risk stakes should carry uncertainty or hope, not sadness.
- **How often to escalate:** the only explicit cadence is the memo (about every 3 min). No sample-based analysis (1of10, VidIQ and others) of stake frequency was found.

### 3.C What happens to retention after a payoff, and how creators handle it

- **Ending abruptly** (PRACTITIONER): MrBeast. Last to Leave has about 85 s after the winner. The boat video ends within about 45 s of the failure.
- **Sponsor after the payoff** (observed): Veritasium (electricity at 13:20, spider silk at 25:30) and Kurzgesagt (trees at 9:35). Viewers who leave after the payoff skip only the ad. No data on its effect on the retention curve was found.
- **Chain a new loop right after a payoff** (observed): Kurzgesagt, 0:30 answer → "only half of the story" → 5:00 answer → "only half of the story" → 7:30 "even wilder". This is a periodic-payoff design: each answer opens the next question.
- **Mid-video payoff, then new stakes** (observed): the boat "float test" at 12:00 ("first time I felt this is actually possible") is followed immediately by new stakes at 13:00 (water, bites, sail still missing).
- No tier 1–3 source shows a retention graph around a payoff moment. The YouTube Help page only says that a spike *can* mark a valued segment, and that a late "top moment" means it should move earlier.

### 3.D Negative and contested (item 3)

- **"Reward schedules" / variable-ratio language:** no tier 1–4 source tests reward schedules (Ferster and Skinner style) on video retention. Analysts' use of the term is metaphor. The defensible nearby evidence is curiosity as reward anticipation (Kang 2009; Gruber 2014). Tag any "reward schedule" rule THEORY at best.
- **Payoff last vs. front-load:** the memo says both. Front-load the *hook and highlights* (first minute) and save the *outcome* for the end. YouTube's guidance ("introduce compelling content earlier") pushes the same way for highlights. Withholding the *answer* is not the same as withholding the *good footage*.
- **Spoiling the payoff in a cold open:** contested (3.10).
- **Failed payoffs:** the boat video ends in failure (94%). One video's views are not evidence. Whether failure endings hurt retention or satisfaction on YouTube is unsourced.
- **Late payoff in explainers vs. early answer:** Veritasium and Harris answer at 79–96%. Kurzgesagt answers at 4% and chains loops. No comparative data. Boyd 2020's TED finding (no mid-story tension peak) suggests explainers need not follow a single climax.
