**How these differ from yours (claim 6.2.x):**
- 6.2.1 Every analyst model above keeps structure, topic/angle, hook and visual delivery on **separate axes** (Sandcastles' own analysis fields; 1of10 format vs interest topic; Chorianopoulos embodiment × media). Your format list mixes four axes in one enum: structure (`levels`, `listicle`, `reveal`, `problem_solution`, `heros_journey`), angle/intent (`common_trap_mistake`, `scenario_hypothetical`, `personal_update`), delivery (`yap`, `reaction`, `interview_clips`, `pov`, `skit_humor`) and series packaging (`episodic_series_social_show`). PRACTITIONER/THEORY. Sources: rows above.
- 6.2.2 Analysts' formats are named by **promise** ("I tried every…", "$1 vs $100", "if I started over", "last to leave"); yours are named by genre. A promise-level format is what a title can carry; Paddy: an idea that cannot be put in a title and thumbnail is probably not a strong idea ([#209, ~24:35](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/paddy-galloway-2/), 2024). PRACTITIONER.
- 6.2.3 Recommendation that follows: split the `storytelling_format` enum into `structure` (beat template the agent checks) + `angle` (promise) + `delivery` (editing style) + `series` (yes/no, numbered or not). The agent can then check a draft against `structure` without arguing over genre labels. Inference from 6.2.1–6.2.2, not sourced as such.
