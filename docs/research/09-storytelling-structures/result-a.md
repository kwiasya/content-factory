# 09 · Storytelling structures that hold attention

Source: Claude Opus research agents (3 researchers, 3 fact-checkers, 1 writer), 2026-10-12. Notes: notes/.

How to read this file:
- Tags: **DATA** = measurement over a sample, A/B test or retention/replay graph shown. **PRACTITIONER** = stated practice, no data shown (platform help pages with no data shown are tagged PRACTITIONER, tier 1). **THEORY** = research or a model not tested on platform video. Text-corpus and lab studies on written stories are THEORY even when they measure something.
- "(transcript)" timestamps come from YouTube caption tracks pulled 2026-10-12, bucketed to 15–60 s. "(chapters)" timestamps come from the video's chapter list (some are YouTube auto-chapters). "(heatmap)" means YouTube's "Most replayed" graph: it measures rewatching and seeking, **not** drop-off. Beat labels on timestamps ("midpoint", "low point") are the researchers' mapping, not the creator's words.
- The fact-checkers could not re-pull captions for the videos in Table A rows 1–9 and Section D (YouTube bot check), so those transcript timestamps are as the researcher recorded them. Chapters and heatmaps in Section B were re-pulled and match.
- Items older than 2024 are flagged "(pre-2024)" where they concern platforms or creator practice.
- Notes: [frameworks-stakes-payoff.md](notes/frameworks-stakes-payoff.md) (items 1–3), [by-length-and-no-narration.md](notes/by-length-and-no-narration.md) (items 4–5), [taxonomy-craft-myths.md](notes/taxonomy-craft-myths.md) (items 6–8).

Notes on Table A:
- **Structure adherence is not a success predictor** (Boyd 2020, DATA on text, negative). [Reagan et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-016-0093-1) found "Icarus", "Oedipus" and double "Man in a hole" arcs had the highest median Gutenberg downloads, but downloads "are only a rough proxy" (THEORY). Use a framework as a check for causality and escalation, not as a forecast.
- **Fiction arcs do not transfer one-to-one to explainers**: TED and NYT science texts lack the mid-story tension peak (Boyd 2020, THEORY). Explainers carry tension as open questions.
- **Pace near the end matters in film and TV**: semantically faster films and episodes get higher IMDb ratings (+0.048 and +0.072 per SD); episodes that cover more ground get lower ratings (−0.082); for TV both effects come from the episode's **end** ([Toubia, Berger & Eliashberg 2021, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2011695118); DATA on subtitles; 4,118 films, 12,401 episodes).

### D.2 Stakes rules

| # | Rule | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-S1 | Raise stakes as the video goes deeper ("stair stepping"); end on the biggest item. | PRACTITIONER | [MrBeast memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/) |
| D-S2 | Hold viewers with an outcome question answered only at the end ("Will they catch me? … Gotta watch until the end."). | PRACTITIONER | memo |

| D-S7 | Front-load: the first minute has the most loss (21M of ~60M clicks in one video); go "above and beyond"; the memo's example cranes a house in "30 seconds into the video" of its 100-days-in-the-circle video. | PRACTITIONER with one internal number | memo pp. 7, 10 |
