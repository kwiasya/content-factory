### 1b. What the opening must do (synthesis; each line rests on the claims cited)

1. **Confirm the click in the first frame or first line.** Show or say the thing from the thumbnail and title (1.1, 1.5, 1.7, 1.16). YouTube judges this at 30 s for long-form (1.1); the MrBeast doc treats the first 60 s as the confirmation window (1.8).
2. **How fast:** about 1.5–3 s on short-form feeds (1.10–1.13); a stated premise in the first line for long-form (1.16). Exact long-form seconds are not measured by any source here (see Gaps).
3. **Match across channels.** Thumbnail, title, first frame, first spoken line and on-screen text must point at one subject (1.7, 1.14, 1.15).
4. **Reward, not preamble.** "The first act has to be a reward" (1.5); "Stop telling... start showing" from minute 1 (1.8).
5. **Pay off any open loop.** A gap that is never closed is read as cheating (1.18, 1.20).

## Gaps

1. **No video-level DATA on hook categories.** No source found compares retention or swipe rate by hook category on YouTube, TikTok or Reels with the sample shown. All category evidence is PRACTITIONER, and the only DATA is from text headlines (D1–D3). Re-mining the Upworthy archive (D3), or the author's own A/B tests (1.2), is the way to get DATA.
2. **Paddy Galloway, Jenny Hoyos, Colin and Samir, Hook Genius, 1of10, Film Booth (2024–2026)** were not reached first-hand. The web-search budget ran out after 14 queries (the task asked for ≥15). 3. **No measured long-form "how fast".** Sources state 0–3 s for short-form (with three different numbers: 1.5, 3 and 5 s) and use 30 s (YouTube's metric) or 60 s (MrBeast doc) as windows for long-form. None measures how many seconds into a long-form video the thumbnail promise must be paid off.
4. **YouTube Help on "Viewed vs swiped away"** (Shorts) was not opened; definitions seen were second-hand. **Mosseri on skip rate** was not found.
5. **The literal "POV:" label** has no source, positive or negative.
