### 6. Negative and contested

- **How much the hook matters is contested, even among hook teachers.** Kallaway rates "hooks are the most important thing" as B-tier advice: topic and angle come first, and "a great topic with an average hook" can still go viral ([2026-09-03, 5:24–6:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SJ0OtNuUzls)). He also calls "your hooks are bad" a lie when the real problem is topic or take ([2026-05-25, 5:35–6:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DFSU-YPRTs8)). Colin and Samir likewise put "is the idea worth watching" before intro craft ([2025-07-16, 24:29–24:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w52xUmjIZPk)). Against this, DiGiovanni (6.5) and Hoyos (6.2) treat the hook as worth hours of work or as a reason to change the idea. For the agent: critique the idea and angle before polishing hook wording. [verified: Kallaway 5:26–6:27 (SJ0OtNuUzls) and 5:32–5:49 (DFSU-YPRTs8); Colin and Samir 24:40–24:46]
- **LLM-as-judge.** Kallaway's loop (6.18) grades hooks with an LLM rubric. 6.24 shows LLM judges are least reliable exactly where human taste varies. Treat the rubric grade as a filter, not a ranking, and keep a human pick or a platform test (6.12–6.13) as the decider.
- **Conflict of interest.** Kallaway sells Sandcastles, which runs Hook Genius and the MCP his workflow requires. His winner/loser percentages are asserted with very small n (4–5 winners per channel) and no graph.
- **Testing optimises for watch time, not truthfulness.** Test & compare picks by watch time (6.12), and Upworthy picked by clicks (6.16). A variant can win a test and still sit badly with the satisfaction and clickbait rules in 7.1–7.4. The Upworthy style that won its tests was later demoted (7.13).
- **The MrBeast doc** is a leak. Passionfruit says two ex-producers confirmed it, but MrBeast's team never did. It dates from 2024, so treat its timings as possibly outdated.

### 7. Negative and contested

- **Curiosity hooks vs Meta's "arbitrary curiosity gap".** Meta penalises withholding *information needed to understand what the video is* (7.10, 7.11), not delayed payoff as such. Practitioners still build on a stated promise with a delayed payoff (6.1, 6.7). A defensible line for the critique checklist: the hook must say *what the video is* (the subject), and only the *outcome* may be withheld. This matches Kallaway's "subject in the first sentence" (6.11).
- **Question hooks are contested.** Film Booth (2022) recommends asking the viewer a question in the intro to start their internal dialogue ([Nb9cQmHFLTs, 8:23–8:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nb9cQmHFLTs), **[old]**). Kallaway's 2026 cross-channel read says hedged questions lose to declaratives (7.16). Neither shows data. The likely reconciliation is that a relatable "do you X?" question differs from a hedged "is X the best?" question, but no source makes that distinction. [verified: Film Booth 8:32–8:36, video dated 2022-08-01]
- **"In this video we'll talk about…" is not always bad.** A compilation guest who runs a "YouTuber Academy" (by my inference Ali Abdaal; he is not named in the clip) says an educational video needs only "a title, a thumbnail, and a list". He "doesn't even like the word hook", calls it "just a promise", and models the opener "In this video, we're going to talk about 17 purchases that save me time" ([Colin and Samir, "The YouTube Playbook in 37 Minutes", 10:19–10:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8XSssH3B_Dk), 2025-08-23). This contradicts a blanket ban on "throat-clearing" (7.16) for list-format education. [verified: 10:01–10:37 in the captions; video dated 2025-08-23; the speaker is not named in the clip]
- **Negativity.** It raises CTR (7.14, DATA), but YouTube's survey-weighted "valued watch time" (7.3) and Meta's sensationalism rule (7.10) push the other way. No source measures the net effect of negative hooks on video satisfaction or long-term reach.
- **"Watch till the end".** This is not banned outright: Meta allows it when the payoff is genuinely meaningful (7.9), and TikTok targets *false* incentives (7.6).
- **Scope of YouTube enforcement.** Egregious-clickbait removal was announced for India, focused on news and current events (7.2). Whether it expanded to other countries in 2025–2026 was not confirmed. The global "Malicious clickbait" spam rule (7.1) applies everywhere, but only to "maliciously" misleading packaging.

## Gaps

- **Search budget.** The harness's shared web-search limit ran out after 12 queries, short of the target of 15. The rest of the work used direct fetches of known primary pages, platform policy pages, creator channel listings and YouTube auto-caption transcripts.
- **No sourced material from Paddy Galloway, Jon Youshaei, vidIQ or 1of10** on hook iteration or hook fatigue. The vidIQ blog returned 403, no Paddy Galloway channel was found under the handles tried, and the 1of10 blog index had no hook posts.
- **"Write 10, pick 1"** has no sourced ratio. The closest are Kallaway keeping about 2 of roughly 20 LLM hooks (anecdote) and Upworthy testing every article. The often-cited Upworthy "25 headlines per post" practice did not appear in the sources fetched.
- **Testing hooks as Community posts:** no source found. **Instagram Trial Reels:** the official page could not be retrieved (404s), so it is unsourced here.
- **YouTube video A/B testing conflicts across sources.** Creator Insider's description says Shorts cuts (6.13); The Publish Press says up to 3 long-form cuts (6.14). The comparison metric is not stated, and the launch is "next year" (2027), so no performance data exists yet. (Fact-check note: the Creator Insider video itself says at 3:15–3:29 "short or long form" and "a breakdown of watch time retention curves", so the format conflict is resolved; the winner metric is still not stated.)
- **Phrase-level spam list for 2025–2026** ("POV:", "Wait for it", "Nobody is talking about this", "Stop scrolling", "You're doing X wrong"): no tier 1–4 source measures fatigue for any specific phrase. Only marketing blogs claim it, and they are excluded. TikTok's and Meta's rules target mechanisms (false incentives, withheld information, shock effects), not phrases.
- **Meta sources.** The original watchbait page and the Transparency Center "engagement bait" page are offline or JS-only, so 7.10 relies on 2022 trade press. Instagram's Recommendation Guidelines (help.instagram.com) could not be retrieved. Meta's 2014 and 2016 clickbait posts are Facebook-feed specific and old.
- **"MrBeast now preplans his first words"** appears only in a [Substack summary](https://paulfoh.substack.com/p/the-formula-for-outstanding-youtube) (2024-10-08) that names no origin, so it was left out of the claims.
- **The Doshi & Hauser sample size** was not retrieved (the *Science Advances* page returned 403; the arXiv abstract gives no N).
- **Text vs video.** Upworthy and Robertson (6.16, 7.14) measure text headline CTR, not spoken or visual video hooks. Transfer to video is assumed, not shown.
